## 0. The diagnosis in one paragraph
The current homepage has the right *ideas* (CPAs work backwards; the $135K counter; real testimonials; Drew) but tells them in the wrong order and at the wrong altitude. It opens with a meadow, a video and a generic promise. It shows the enemy ("Most CPAs work backwards") before it shows the product. It leans on stats that hurt trust ("100% Success Rate" and "$50K" with no unit, both heavily disclaimed). It never explains *what you get*, *who does the work*, or *what it costs to commit*. The CTA vocabulary is scattered (Get Started / Take The Assessment / Unlock Your Savings Now! / Start The Game), and "Get Started" and "Take The Assessment" go to different URLs. A $100M fintech homepage does the opposite. It makes one clear promise, shows the product early, answers "is this legit?" before you ask, and repeats one CTA.

### The white space
Everyone else is one of these:
1. **Filing / back office** (Keeper, Collective, Pilot, Bench): looks backward, finds write-offs after the fact.
2. **Software for CPAs** (Instead, TaxPlanIQ): the strategy exists, but the owner can't get it directly.
3. **Seminar / call-gated advisors** (Anderson, Tax Hive): loud, guarantee-led, and you can't see anything until a salesperson calls you.

**Nobody owns this combination:**
> A *self-serve way in* (TaxLand shows your likely strategies in minutes, no call required) → a *strategy-first* plan built on the high-net-worth playbook → *implemented with you during the year* → *filed by licensed pros*. All of it fronted by a founder with a real audience and an honest, anti-hype voice.

**HeyDrew's unfair advantages:** (a) the assessment is a product experience, not a lead form; (b) Andrew's personal brand and stage audience (Create / Keep / Multiply); (c) Drew, an ownable character that no fintech competitor can copy; (d) the honesty stance ("No, we can't guarantee savings") in a category full of guarantees.

## 2. Positioning

**Positioning statement**
> For business owners who make real money and suspect they're overpaying, **HeyDrew** is the **year-round tax strategy service** that plans your taxes *while the year is still open*. AI finds the moves you qualify for, and licensed CPAs help you put them in place and file. Unlike tax prep and bookkeeping services that record what already happened, HeyDrew works on the part you can still change.

**Category to claim:** **Year-round tax strategy** (short form: *"Tax strategy, not tax prep."*)
- It's searchable and self-explanatory ("tax strategy for business owners" is how Andrew is already known).
- It creates a clean enemy category, **tax prep** (backward, April, reactive), without attacking CPAs as people. HeyDrew *has* CPAs.
- Branded frame inside the category: **"The Keep system"** (Andrew's Create · **Keep** · Multiply). Use it in the founder section and in sub-copy, not as the headline category.

**Core promise:** *We find every legitimate strategy you qualify for, help you set it up during the year, and show you the math.* This comes straight from the FAQ: "What we guarantee is that we'll look at every strategy you legitimately qualify for and show you the math." That is the honest version of a guarantee, and it's the strongest line HeyDrew owns.

**Brand line (unchanged, elevated):** *You created the income. HeyDrew helps you keep it.*

Augusta Rule rental days (§280A, up to 14 days) have to actually happen and be documented. Accountable-plan reimbursements need a plan in place. Solo 401(k) setup and contribution deadlines. "By then, the savings are already gone" is a factual claim here, not hype.

- **Line:** *"April is a history lesson. We work on the part of the year you can still change."*

*"AI-Driven, Human-Perfected," made concrete.*
- **Proof:** The TaxLand assessment surfaces the strategies you likely qualify for, with no call needed. - **Line:** *"Software does the finding. People you can text do the rest."*

## 6. How Andrew and Drew each show up

**Division of labor:** **Andrew = authority** (real human, real photo, real story, accountable). **Drew = guidance** (points you to the next step, celebrates progress, jokes about the *system*). Andrew makes the claims; Drew never does.

### Andrew (founder authority)
- One dedicated **founder section** with a real photo (stage or studio) [PLACEHOLDER - confirm photo + rights], a short first-person note, and his signature.
- Named as a byline in the FAQ answer "Who's behind HeyDrew?".
- **Do not use on the homepage:** "America's #1 Tax Strategist" (an unverifiable superlative), "$300M sold from stage" (irrelevant to the client and it reads salesy). Put them on /about if legal clears them.
- Optional: a 60–90s founder video in the founder section only (not the hero). Lazy-loaded with a poster frame. [PLACEHOLDER - confirm new cut; current welcome video is 2:52]

### Drew: brand elevation rules (making the mascot feel premium)
1. **Guide, not wallpaper.** Drew appears at a maximum of **4 moments**: hero (pointing at the product), the stakes counter (herald peeking over the card), the offer band (holding the map), and success/empty states. No meadow backgrounds behind page sections.
2. **TaxLand lives inside the product frame.** The storybook world is shown *inside* a device or app card: it's the place you enter when you click the CTA. The site itself is clean white/navy fintech. This one rule fixes the "cartoon website" feeling while keeping the equity.
3. **Scale ceiling.** Drew never takes up more than ~35% of a section's width and never sits behind or under the headline. Type leads; Drew supports.
4. **Cut-out on calm grounds.** Renders go on white, #F4F6F8, or navy, with one soft grounding shadow. Same key light every time (warm, top-left). No mixed lighting from different renders side by side.
5. **Always pointing at something real:** a number, a strategy card, or the CTA. Drew is a pointer, and the user is the hero.
6. **Speaks in captions of 8 words or fewer.** Examples: "This one's worth a look." "Your turn." "The IRS built this in." He never says numbers, guarantees or legal info.
7. **Retire Sad Drew on the homepage.** Loss is shown with data (the timeline), not a pouting mascot.
8. **Motion is subtle.** One slow float (4–6s, 6px amplitude). Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. No auto-playing GIFs.
9. **Wardrobe lock.** Backwards black cap, black puffer vest, white hoodie, gold watch, beaded bracelets in every render (see brand kit §2.2).
10. **Mischief points at the IRS or the "April scramble," never at the client's money.**
