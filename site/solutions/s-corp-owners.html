What this split means

Salary that matches the work.

You pay yourself what the market pays for your role, through payroll. Profit above that can come out as distributions.

Salary (W-2)
Runs through payroll. Social Security and Medicare taxes apply. The IRS expects a reasonable salary before distributions.
Distributions
Profit paid to you as an owner. No payroll taxes on it, as long as your salary holds up.

Illustrative scenario. There is no fixed percentage. A reasonable salary depends on your role, hours, industry and what the market pays for that work, and we document how we got to it. Your results depend on your situation and are not guaranteed.

The Accountable Plan

Your company reimburses you for real business costs you pay personally, like a home office, phone and mileage. The company deducts them, and you don't pay income tax on the reimbursement.

How the Accountable Plan works

The Augusta Rule

Your company rents your home for up to 14 days a year for real business use, like board meetings or planning days. The company deducts the rent, and that rent can be tax-free to you.

How the Augusta Rule works

Retirement that matches your salary

Your W-2 salary helps set how much the company can put into a Solo 401(k) for you. So we plan the salary and the retirement contribution together, not in separate meetings.
