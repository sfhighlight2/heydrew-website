How we approach real estate

Real estate rules are some of the most detailed in the tax code. We'll tell you what applies to you, and what doesn't.

  • We start from your facts, not a pitch
  • Topics to explore, never promises
  • Licensed pros handle what needs a license

Open the property file.

What we look at with owners who hold real estate. Each tab covers what it is, what we'll ask you and what to keep on file.

Entity structure

How you hold a property shapes your liability, how its income is reported and what's possible later. Holding it personally, in an LLC or in a partnership each trade off differently, and your state's rules matter too.

We map what you own today and whether the structure still fits where you're headed.

Questions we'll ask

  • Who owns each property today, and in what name?
  • Is it a rental, your business's space or your home?
  • Are partners, a spouse or family involved?
  • Are you planning to buy, sell or refinance soon?

Keep on file

  • Deeds and closing statements
  • Operating or partnership agreements
  • Leases, including any with your own business
  • A separate bank account for each entity

The Augusta Rule

If you own a home and run a business, your company may rent your home for up to 14 days a year for real business use, like meetings or planning days. The business deducts a fair rent, and that rent can be tax-free to you.

It's about the home you live in, not your rental properties.

How the Augusta Rule works

Questions we'll ask

  • Does your business hold meetings that could happen at your home?
  • What would a comparable local venue charge?
  • Is your business set up to pay you rent?

Keep on file

  • Comparable rates for similar spaces
  • A rental agreement and an invoice
  • Agendas, minutes and who attended
  • Payment from the business account

Real estate professional status

This status can change how rental losses are treated on your return. The tests are strict. They turn on how many hours you spend on real estate and how much of your working time it takes up.

It's a topic to explore with the team, not something to assume. We'll look at your hours and your records honestly, including if the answer is no.

Explore, don't assume

Questions we'll ask

  • How many hours a year do you really spend on your properties?
  • Is real estate your main work, or alongside another job?
  • Does a spouse work in real estate?
  • Are you tracking your time today?

Keep on file

  • A time log kept as you go, not rebuilt later
  • Calendars, emails and invoices that back it up
  • Which properties you actively manage

Depreciation & cost segregation

Rental and business buildings are usually depreciated over many years. Land isn't. A cost segregation study splits a property into parts that may be depreciated faster.

Whether a study is worth it depends on the property, how long you'll hold it and what happens when you sell. So we talk it through before anyone orders one.

A topic to discuss

Questions we'll ask

  • When did the property go into service, and what did it cost?
  • How much of the price was land versus building?
  • What have you improved since?
  • How long do you plan to keep it?

Keep on file

  • Closing statements
  • Improvement receipts, kept apart from repairs
  • Prior depreciation schedules
  • Any appraisal or study already done

Records

Good records are what make every real estate position hold up. Mixed accounts and missing receipts make everything else harder, and they're the easiest thing to fix early in the year.

Bookkeeping & payroll

Questions we'll ask

  • Does each property or entity have its own account?
  • Are repairs and improvements tracked separately?
  • Who keeps the books today?

Keep on file

  • Leases and rent records
  • Mileage for property trips
  • Receipts sorted by property
  • Year-end statements for each account

Educational only. Whether any of this applies depends on your properties, your work and your state. We'll tell you plainly on your call.
