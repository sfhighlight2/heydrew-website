Hire Your Kids

Pay your kids real wages for real work. The business deducts it, and they may owe little or no income tax on it.

S-Corp ownersSelf-employedFamilies

Good fit ifyour kids can do real, age-appropriate work in the business.

How it works

Your kids do real work for the business, and you pay them a fair wage for it through payroll. The business deducts the wages like any other payroll.

Up to the standard deduction ($16,100 for 2026), your child may owe no federal income tax on those wages. Whether payroll taxes apply depends on how your business is set up, so we check your entity first.

Planning for families

What to keep on file

  • A written job description
  • Timesheets for the hours worked
  • A rate you'd pay anyone for that work
  • Payroll records and a W-2
  • Pay deposited in an account in their name

Augusta Rule

Rent your home to your business for up to 14 days a year for real business use.

S-Corp ownersHomeowners

Good fit ifyou own your home and hold real meetings or events there for your business.

How it works

Your business rents your home for real business use, like board meetings, planning days or team events, for up to 14 days a year. It pays a fair rate, the kind a comparable local venue would charge.

The business deducts the rent. Because the home is rented for 14 days or fewer, that rent can be tax-free to you. Your business needs to be set up so it can pay you rent, which we check first.

Planning for S-Corp owners

What to keep on file

  • Rates for comparable local spaces
  • A rental agreement and invoice for each use
  • Agendas, minutes and who attended
  • Payment from the business account

Solo 401(k)

Put away far more than an IRA allows, pre-tax or Roth.

Self-employedS-Corp owners

Good fit ifyou're self-employed with no full-time staff besides a spouse.

How it works

A retirement plan built for owner-only businesses. You contribute twice: once as the employee, from your pay, and again as the business.

That's how it can hold far more than an IRA. The limits change every year, and for S-Corp owners they're tied to your W-2 salary, so we work out your number with you.

Planning for the self-employed

What to keep on file

  • Plan documents, set up on time
  • Each contribution and when it was made
  • Your W-2 salary or self-employment earnings
  • The plan's annual filing once it's required

Accountable Plan

Your company reimburses real business costs you pay personally, and deducts them.

S-Corp ownersC-Corp owners

Good fit ifyou run an S-Corp or C-Corp and pay for some business costs yourself.

How it works

A written plan lets your company pay you back for business costs you cover personally: the business share of your home office, your phone, mileage and similar costs.

The company deducts the reimbursements, and they aren't taxable income to you, as long as each one has a business purpose, is backed by records and is turned in within a reasonable time.

Planning for S-Corp owners

What to keep on file

  • A resolution adopting the plan
  • Expense reports with receipts
  • A mileage log
  • Home office measurements
  • Reimbursements paid by the business

Health Savings Account

Deductible going in, tax-free growth, tax-free out for medical costs.

Self-employedS-Corp ownersFamilies

Good fit ifyou're covered by a qualifying high-deductible health plan.

How it works

A savings account for medical costs that pairs with a qualifying high-deductible health plan. Money goes in deductible or pre-tax, grows tax-free, and comes out tax-free for qualified medical costs.

It isn't use-it-or-lose-it: the balance stays yours from year to year. S-Corp owners run contributions through payroll in a specific way, which we set up with you.

Planning for the self-employed

What to keep on file

  • Proof of your high-deductible coverage
  • A record of each contribution
  • Receipts for every medical withdrawal

Health Reimbursement Arrangement

Your business pays medical costs with pre-tax dollars instead of you paying with after-tax dollars.

FamiliesS-Corp ownersSelf-employed

Good fit ifyou can meet the eligibility rules, often through a spouse who works in the business.

How it works

A written arrangement lets your business pay back qualified medical costs for eligible employees. The business deducts what it pays, and the reimbursements are tax-free to the employee.

Who can take part depends on your entity type and who's on payroll. For many owners it works when a spouse is a real employee of the business, so we check eligibility before anything else.

Planning for families

What to keep on file

  • The written plan document
  • An employment agreement for the employee
  • Receipts for each medical expense
  • A record of each reimbursement

Traditional & Roth IRA

Pick the right account in the right year. Over a lifetime, the difference can be big.

Almost everyoneWorking kids

Good fit foralmost everyone with earned income, including kids who work in the business.

How it works

Two accounts with opposite timing. A Traditional IRA may give you a deduction now, and you pay tax when the money comes out. A Roth IRA gives no deduction now, but qualified withdrawals later are tax-free.

Which one wins depends on your tax rate now versus later, and income limits can change what you're allowed to do. We look at it every year, not once.

Roth IRAs for working kids

What to keep on file

  • Contribution records and year-end forms
  • Any contributions you didn't deduct
  • Records of any Roth conversions

529 Education Plan

Tax-free growth and tax-free qualified withdrawals, plus a state deduction in many states.

Families

Good fit ifyou're saving for school.

How it works

An education savings plan. Contributions grow tax-free, and withdrawals for qualified education costs are tax-free too.

Many states add a deduction or credit for contributing to their plan. Starting early gives the money the most time to grow.

Planning for families

What to keep on file

  • Plan statements
  • Receipts for qualified education costs
  • Withdrawals matched to costs in the same year
