Your toolkit, before any paperwork changes.

You don't need to be a corporation to plan. A few of the moves we look at first for solo owners. Eligibility depends on your situation.

Solo 401(k)

You contribute as the employee and again as the business, so you can put away far more than an IRA allows. Pre-tax or Roth, depending on the plan.

Good fit if you're self-employed with no full-time staff besides a spouse.

How it works

Health Savings Account

Deductible going in, tax-free growth, tax-free out for medical costs. The money stays yours year to year.

Good fit if you're on a qualifying high-deductible plan.

How it works

Hire Your Kids

Pay your kids real wages for real work. Your business deducts it, and up to the standard deduction ($16,100 for 2026) they may owe no income tax on it.

How it works
Home office, today

Deduct the space you really use

As a sole prop, a qualifying home office is deducted right on your own return. The space has to be used regularly, and only for the business.

If you elect S-Corp

Reimburse it through an Accountable Plan

Your company pays you back for the business share of your home, phone and mileage, and deducts it.
