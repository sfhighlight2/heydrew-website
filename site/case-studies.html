How does a tax plan get built?Four illustrative scenarios.

A Tax Valet asks about your business, explores the strategies that might fit and documents each move. These owners don't exist; the questions do. Pick one.

Scenario A

Illustrative scenario: an S-Corp consultant

Illustrative scenario, not a client

1Before

A one-person consulting firm taxed as an S-Corp, run from a dedicated room at home, with a planning day each quarter. Files on time, but nobody has planned anything.

5 questions the Tax Valet asks
  1. How did you set your salary, and when was it last reviewed?
  2. Which business costs are you paying personally right now?
  3. Is there a room at home used only for the business?
  4. Where do your quarterly planning days happen today?
  5. What have you put toward retirement so far this year?

2Strategies explored

  • Accountable PlanLikely fitReimburses home office, phone and mileage paid personally.
  • Augusta RuleWorth a lookRent the home for real meetings, 14 days a year at most.
  • Solo 401(k)Likely fitNo employees besides the owner. Timing rules apply.
  • Owner salaryReviewA reasonable salary is a strategy decision.

3On paper

  • A signed Accountable Plan resolution
    Get the template
  • Monthly reimbursement reports and mileage logs
  • A comparable rent worksheet for the meeting space
    Get the worksheet
  • An agenda and notes for each meeting day
  • Retirement plan paperwork and a contribution schedule

Scenario B

Illustrative scenario: a family business with working kids

Illustrative scenario, not a client

1Before

Married co-owners of a small online shop. Their two teenagers pack orders and run the social accounts, paid now and then in cash, with no records.

5 questions the Tax Valet asks
  1. How old is each child, and what work do they actually do?
  2. How many hours a week, and what would you pay someone else for it?
  3. How is the business set up for tax: sole proprietorship, partnership or S-Corp?
  4. Do the kids have bank accounts in their own names?
  5. What are you already saving for college or retirement?

2Strategies explored

  • Hire Your KidsLikely fitReal wages for real work, paid through payroll.
  • Traditional & Roth IRAWorth a lookKids with earned income can contribute.
  • 529 Education PlanWorth a lookCollege savings. State rules vary.
  • Cash with no recordsUnlikelyUndocumented pay doesn't support a deduction.

3On paper

  • A written job description for each child
  • Timesheets, kept as the work happens
  • Pay rates compared with what the work costs elsewhere
  • Payroll records and year-end wage forms
  • Wages paid into the kids' own accounts

Scenario C

Illustrative scenario: an owner who also holds rentals

Illustrative scenario, not a client

1Before

Owns an operating business taxed as an S-Corp and, separately, two long-term rentals titled in the owners' names. The business books are clean. The rentals live in a spreadsheet.

5 questions the Tax Valet asks
  1. How is each property titled, and is it insured to match?
  2. Who manages the rentals, and roughly how many hours go into them?
  3. Do you have purchase and improvement records for each property?
  4. Is anything being bought, sold or refinanced this year?
  5. Does the business ever meet at your home?

2Strategies explored

  • Property recordsReviewClean books per property come first.
  • How the properties are heldReviewTitling affects liability and taxes. Bring an attorney.
  • Real estate professional statusUnlikelyStrict time tests most full-time owners won't meet.
  • Augusta RuleWorth a lookThe owners' own home, for real business meetings.

3On paper

  • A ledger for each property, kept with the bookkeeping
  • Purchase, improvement and depreciation records
  • An hour log, if time spent is part of the decision
  • A comparable rent worksheet for home meetings

Scenario D

Illustrative scenario: a solo creator

Illustrative scenario, not a client

1Before

A single-member LLC paid through sponsorships, platform payouts and affiliate links, in bursts. Works from a home studio, has a high-deductible health plan and no retirement account yet.

5 questions the Tax Valet asks
  1. Where does income arrive, and how is it tracked today?
  2. Is the LLC taxed as a sole proprietorship, or has it elected S-Corp status?
  3. How are you handling estimated tax payments on uneven income?
  4. Is your health plan HSA-eligible?
  5. What gear and studio costs did you pay for this year, and do you have receipts?

2Strategies explored

  • Solo 401(k)Likely fitContributions can flex with uneven income.
  • Health Savings AccountLikely fitIf the health plan is HSA-eligible.
  • S-Corp electionReviewWorth it at some incomes, not others. Run the math.
  • Accountable PlanReviewOnly relevant after an S-Corp election.

3On paper

  • Bookkeeping categories for each income source
  • A quarterly estimated tax schedule
  • Retirement and HSA account paperwork
  • Receipts and business-use notes for gear and the studio
