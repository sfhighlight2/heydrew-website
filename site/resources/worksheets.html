Augusta Rule Comparable (FMV) Worksheet

How do I document a fair rent for the Augusta Rule?

Use the comparable (FMV) worksheet. It records what similar local venues charge, so the daily rent your business pays you for meetings at your home is backed by real comparables.

What it’s for
Supporting the fair market daily rate for renting your home to your business. IRC §280A(g) requires the rent to be at fair market value, and the worksheet asks for at least three comparable rates with screenshots.
When you’d use it
Before the first rental day of the year, and again if your rate changes. Research the comparables first, then set the rate.
What you’ll need
  • Your home address (it must be your primary residence) and the tax year
  • The number of rental days you plan, up to 14
  • At least three local venues: source or URL, name, location, capacity and daily rate
  • Screenshots of each comparable
  • Your proposed daily rate, and a reason if it differs from the average
  • The preparer’s name, title and signature

Accountable Plan Board Resolution / Owner Resolution

How do I adopt an accountable plan?

An accountable plan is a written policy that lets your business reimburse business costs you paid personally. This template adopts it formally, before the first reimbursement.

What it’s for
Putting the plan on the record. It comes in two versions: Version A for a board or multiple owners (S-Corp, C-Corp or multi-member LLC), and Version B for a sole proprietor or single owner with no board. Complete only the one that applies.
When you’d use it
Before reimbursements start. The resolution sets the plan’s effective date; reimbursements need a business connection, receipts, and any excess returned to the company. IRS Publication 463 covers how reimbursement plans are treated.
What you’ll need
  • Entity name, entity type and state of organization
  • The date of adoption and the plan’s effective date
  • Version A: the name, title and signature of each board member or owner
  • Version B: the owner’s name and the business name

Use these two together

What paperwork does an HRA need?

A health reimbursement arrangement (HRA) needs two separate documents: a written plan that sets the rules, and an employment agreement that documents a real job. They stay separate on purpose.

PDF PDF

HRA Written Plan Document

What it’s for
The plan itself: who’s eligible, the annual maximum, covered expenses and how claims work. Without it, reimbursements can be reclassified as taxable income.
When you’d use it
Before any reimbursement is paid, usually at the start of the plan year.
What you’ll need
  • Plan name, employer name, address and EIN
  • Plan effective date and plan year
  • Which employees are eligible, and any waiting period
  • The annual maximum per employee, and whether unused amounts carry over
  • Covered persons, claim deadline and who administers the plan

HRA Employment Agreement

What it’s for
Documenting a real job with specific duties, pay and hours. It only notes that the employee may join the HRA; the plan terms stay in the plan document.
When you’d use it
When the employee starts, or before they join the plan.
What you’ll need
  • Employer and employee details, and the supervisor
  • Job title, full- or part-time status and weekly hours
  • The pay rate, run through regular payroll
  • A specific description of the duties, not generic language
  • Signatures from both employer and employee
